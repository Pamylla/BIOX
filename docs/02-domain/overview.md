## Why this document comes first

Before asking "what is a Patient?", we need to ask "what is the **language** of BIOX?".

The names defined here don't stay on paper — they show up in database tables, API endpoints, backend services, UI components, tests, and even the AI prompts. A poorly chosen term now is carried through the entire project. This is the **Ubiquitous Language** of BIOX: a single vocabulary, used the same way by everyone and across every layer.

---

## Ubiquitous Language — the official glossary

Each term has a definition and, just as important, what it is **not** — the boundary that keeps the vocabulary from drifting over time.

### User
The authenticatable identity. Handles login and access.
**It is not** the clinical data (that's `Patient`). A `User` authenticates; a `Patient` has a health history. In the MVP the relationship is 1:1.

### Patient
The monitored person and their clinical data.
**It is not** the login account (that's `User`). In the MVP, one `User` maps to one `Patient`.

### Extraction
An uploaded lab report file and the process of extracting data from it.
**It is not** the clinical event. It is the *upload* — the PDF, the parsing, the review. A single `Extraction` may yield results from different dates.

### Batch
A clinical event: the set of measurements from a single collection date.
**It is not** the file. It is the "commit" of the health state — the dated version. A `Batch` may come from one or more `Extractions`.

> **Why `Extraction` and `Batch` instead of "Exam"?** Because "exam" conflates two things that need to live separately: the file that arrives and the clinical event it represents. A PDF may contain exams from different dates; an event may come from two PDFs. A single term would reintroduce that ambiguity across every layer.

### Biomarker
The global definition of a marker — ferritin, CRP, TSH.
**It is not** a value. It is the concept: name, canonical unit, synonyms, domain, clinical direction. It exists once in the system, in the catalog. The measured value is a `Measurement`.

### Measurement
The value of a `Biomarker` measured on a specific date, with the reference range from that report.
**It is not** the marker definition (that's `Biomarker`). It is the atom of the biomarker-oriented database — the datum that repeats with each `Batch` and forms the time series.

> **Why `Measurement` and not "Result" or "Exam Result"?** Because the term emphasizes the *act of measuring* a marker at a point in time — which is what sustains the longitudinal view. A ferritin `Measurement` in January and another in April are the same quantity measured twice; it is that repetition over time that gives the product its value.

### Score
A computed indicator (0–100), per domain and overall, frozen at the moment of confirmation.
**It is not** AI-generated and **it is not** recomputed on every view. It is a deterministic function of the `Measurements`, with a versioned formula. Once computed, it is history.

### Insight
A natural-language explanation generated by AI.
**It is not** diagnosis, prescription, or treatment. It interprets and contextualizes data. It reads `Measurements` and `Scores`; it **never** alters them.

### Report *(deferred — post-MVP)*
A consolidated composition of data for a period.
**It is not** a new source of truth — it is a view over data that already exists. Documented now, built later.

> **A note on registers.** This ubiquitous language governs the domain model, the database, the APIs, the engineering layers, and the AI prompts — everywhere precision matters. Reader-facing narrative in the product docs (`vision.md`, `product-requirements.md`) may use everyday words — *exam*, *result*, *report* — for readability; those map to `Extraction` (the uploaded file), `Batch` (the dated clinical event), and `Measurement` (a measured value), respectively. The term `Report` is reserved for the deferred post-MVP consolidated view and is never used for the uploaded file.

## The data flow

How information travels through the system, from upload to explanation:

```
               User
                  │
      authenticates and manages access
                  │
                  ▼
               Patient
                  │
           uploads laboratory reports
                  ▼
            Extraction ─────────────► (uploaded document and ingestion process)
                  │
        parsing + human review
                  ▼
               Batch ───────────────► (dated clinical event / health snapshot)
                  │
              contains
                  ▼
      Measurement ◄──────── Biomarker
   (measured value)       (global definition)
                  │
        ┌─────────┴─────────┐
        │                   │
        ▼                   ▼
      Score             Insight
(deterministic)      (AI-generated)
        │
        ▼
 Dashboard / Timeline
 (read-only views)
```

The path always moves **forward**. The PDF becomes an `Extraction`, which produces a `Batch`, which contains `Measurements`, which feed `Scores` and `Insights`. Nothing flows back: the `Insight` never rewrites the `Score`, the `Score` never rewrites the `Measurement`.

> The diagram is deliberately simplified. Two read-only relationships are detailed in the per-entity documents rather than drawn here: the **Timeline** reads raw `Measurements` directly (not only through `Score`), and the **Insight** reads the `Score` and curated knowledge as input — always read-only, never writing back.

## The taxonomy of entities

Not everything in the system is of the same nature. Separating this prevents modeling mistakes — like trying to "save the timeline" (which is a computation, not data).

### Permanent — stored as truth
`User`, `Patient`, `Extraction`, `Batch`, `Biomarker`, `Measurement`.
These are the factual record (`User` being the identity that owns the clinical data). Once confirmed, they do not change silently (a correction is a new record, a deletion is a soft-delete).

### Computed — derived from `Measurements` by formula
`Score`.
Deterministic and reproducible. Frozen at confirmation with a formula version, but *derived* — it could be recomputed from the `Measurements` and the formula.

### AI-generated — interpretation, not fact
`Insight`.
The product of a language model. Traceable (it records which data it was based on), historizable, and always a leaf of the flow — it reads, never writes.

### View-only — not an entity
**Timeline**, **trend**, **version diff**.
These are *on-demand computations* over `Measurements` and `Batches`. They have no table. The timeline is a query ordered by date; the trend is a linear regression; the diff is a comparison between two `Batches`. Storing them would duplicate data that already exists.

## The questions this document answers

- **What are the core concepts?** The ones in the glossary above.
- **How do they relate?** Through the data-flow diagram — always forward.
- **What is permanent?** User, Patient, Extraction, Batch, Biomarker, Measurement.
- **What is computed?** Score.
- **What is AI-generated?** Insight.
- **What is view-only?** Timeline, trend, diff — computations, not entities.
