# Biomarker

**Document:** `docs/02-domain/biomarker.md`
**Type:** permanent entity (global catalog)
**Related:** `overview.md`, `measurement.md`, `score.md`, `biomarker-catalog.md` (the instances)

---

## Purpose

Represent the **global definition of a marker** — ferritin, CRP, TSH, weight. `Biomarker` is the concept, not the value: it exists once in the system, in the catalog, and is referenced by all `Measurements` of that marker. It is what guarantees that "ferritin" means the same thing in every exam, for every patient.

While `biomarker.md` describes the *concept* of Biomarker (what it is, its rules), `biomarker-catalog.md` lists the concrete *instances* (the 22 markers of the MVP).

## Responsibilities

- Store the marker's code, name, and canonical unit.
- Store the synonyms the parser uses to recognize the marker in reports with varied spellings.
- Store the primary domain (where it scores) and the secondary ones (context).
- Store the clinical direction (`direction`) of the reading.
- Distinguish a laboratory marker from an anthropometric one.

Not the `Biomarker`'s responsibility: storing values (that's `Measurement`) and storing reference ranges (those come from the report, per result — ADR-002). The `Biomarker` states the *sense* of the reading, never the numbers.

## Relationships

- Belongs to no `Patient` — it is shared, global catalog.
- Referenced by many `Measurements` (the definition ↔ the measured values).
- Its primary domain determines which `Score` the marker scores in.

## Business rules

- **Single definition, never duplicated.** The `Measurement` points to the `Biomarker`; it does not copy its definition. This prevents "ferritin" being defined divergently across exams or patients.
- **Scores in one domain only.** The `primaryDomain` is the sole domain where the marker enters the `Score` computation. The `secondaryDomains[]` are interpretive context — they appear in the reading, but do not score. This invariant is what keeps the five scores independent and auditable (one datum does not inflate two scores at once).
- **`direction` states the sense of the reading**, not the numbers: `high_bad` (higher is worse), `low_bad` (lower is worse — e.g. HDL), `range` (deviation in either direction matters — e.g. TSH), `context` (does not generate a flag on its own; depends on cross-referencing — e.g. ferritin).
- **Laboratory vs anthropometric** is a characteristic of the `Biomarker`. A laboratory marker comes from a report (via `Extraction`); an anthropometric one is manual entry (e.g. weight, via the Profile). Both generate `Measurements` of the same nature (value + date + time series); the origin is what differs.
- **Immutable at runtime.** The catalog is curated, not user-editable. Catalog changes are versioned and deliberate, not generated by usage.

## Multi-domain markers

A marker in more than one domain **is the rule, not the exception**. Three of the 22 MVP markers are multi-domain:

- **Ferritin** — primary Iron, secondary Inflammation (iron store *and* acute-phase reactant).
- **Triglycerides** — primary Metabolic, secondary Cardiovascular.
- **High-sensitivity CRP** — primary Cardiovascular, secondary Inflammation.

The `primaryDomain` + `secondaryDomains[]` pattern resolves all of them uniformly, and it scales: a new marker enters by answering "where does it score?" and "where is it just context?", without touching the architecture.

## Future evolution

- New markers enter through the same pattern, with no structural change. A context marker with no domain in the MVP (e.g. Vitamin D, currently without a score) becomes a scoring one when a new domain is created (e.g. a Vitamins score), with no data migration — just promotion.
- Supports additional definition metadata (e.g. whether the marker admits an assay-method distinction) when clinically meaningful.

## Boundary note

The `Biomarker` is the *definition*; the `Measurement` is the *measured value* of that definition on a date. The `Biomarker` says "what ferritin is and how to read it"; the `Measurement` says "this patient's ferritin on 04/01 was 28, range 30–400". The reference range does **not** live in the `Biomarker` — it always comes from the report (see `measurement.md` and ADR-002).
